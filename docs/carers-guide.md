# Home Dojo: Carers' Guide

The app shows this guide under 📖 Guide, with the children's names and their current behaviours filled in. {1} is the first child in Settings, {2} the second.

## Welcome

Home Dojo gives {1} and {2} a Dojo every time we catch them doing something good, so they hear far more praise than correction. Each child has a monster that grows as Dojos add up.

It works because the praise is quick, specific and consistent between grown-ups. Consistency matters a lot for young kids: when every adult notices the same few behaviours in the same way, they learn what is expected much faster.

Three rules hold everything together:

- Dojos are only ever added, never taken away.
- We reward the behaviour, not the child's mood or the day overall.
- Give the Dojo straight away, with words.

## Quick start

1. Open Home Dojo from your home screen and sign in with the invite a parent sent you.
2. Tap the child's card (or their house in the garden).
3. On the Earn tab, tap the behaviour you just saw. You'll hear a chime and see confetti, and a +1 pops up.
4. Say out loud what they did (see "How to award" below).
5. Tapped the wrong one? Press **Undo** on the message at the bottom. You can also remove your own entry from the Week tab within 15 minutes.

The little dot at the top shows whether everything has synced. If you're offline, Dojos are saved on your phone and sync later.

## Dojos and Dojo $

Every behaviour you reward gives the child one Dojo and one Dojo $ at the same time.

|  | Dojos (blue star) | Dojo $ (gold coin) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | Everything ever earned | Spending money |
| Goes down? | Never | Yes, when they buy something |
| Used for | Monster levels, big reward goals, trophies, mystery eggs, the team jar | The Shop: pets, hats, outfits, house upgrades, furniture, everyday treats |

So a child who spends their Dojo $ never loses progress towards a big reward. If they ask "did I lose my points?", the answer is always no: their Dojos stay the same.

Big reward goals (like a park trip with ice cream) unlock automatically at a Dojos total. A parent marks them as given once they've happened.

## When to award a Dojo

Award a Dojo when you see one of the child's listed behaviours, as it happens or within a minute or two. Aim for roughly 5 to 10 Dojos a day for {1} and 3 to 6 for {2}. That's enough to feel frequent without becoming background noise.

**Rules of thumb**

- **Catch it in the moment.** A Dojo straight away teaches far more than one at the end of the day.
- **Reward effort and recovery, not perfection.** If {1} starts to get upset and then calms himself, that is exactly the moment for "Calm body".
- **Harder moments count more than easy ones.** Stopping a favourite game when the timer goes is worth noticing every time. Sitting quietly while already calm is not.
- **Be generous early in a new routine, then taper.** Once something is easy (for example, every time he stops at the timer without fuss), award it about every other time and add praise words in between.
- **One behaviour, one Dojo.** Don't give several Dojos for one moment. Save the **Wow! Bonus** for something genuinely special that isn't on the list.

**What each behaviour looks like**

{{BEHAVIOURS}}

**Don't award Dojos**

- To stop a meltdown or end an argument (that teaches that upset earns points).
- Because the child asked for one. Notice the behaviour later instead.
- For things already paid with something else, like a treat promised for finishing dinner.

## How to award

Say what they did, then tap. Name the exact behaviour so it links in their head to the Dojo.

- "You stopped as soon as the timer went. That's a Dojo!"
- "You were really cross and you took big breaths. Calm body Dojo."
- "You let {2} have a turn. That was so kind. Kind to {2}!"
- For {2}, keep it short and warm: "Gentle hands! Dojo!" with a smile and a high five.

For {1}, keep your voice calm and the words the same each time. Predictable praise is easier to take in than big excited speeches. If the sound or confetti seems too much for him on a given day, turn the sound off with the speaker button at the top.

Don't add "but" ("Good listening, but next time…"). Let the Dojo stand on its own.

## Tricky moments

| Situation | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| Meltdown or big upset | Leave the app alone and help him regulate first. Once he has calmed, a "Calm body" Dojo for calming down is fine. Never offer Dojos to stop the meltdown. |
| "Can I have a Dojo?" | "Let's see if I spot one!" Then genuinely look for the next chance to catch the behaviour. |
| Bad behaviour | Handle it the usual way. Never remove Dojos or Dojo $, and don't mention the app as a threat. |
| One child has more | Don't compare them. Each child's Dojos are about their own goals. The team jar is the shared one, where every Dojo helps both. |
| Arguing over the tablet | Tap for them, or let the child whose turn it is tap their own button. Turn-taking on the tablet can itself earn "Kind to {2}". |
| You're unsure if it counts | Give it. A slightly generous Dojo does far less harm than a missed one. |

## Little ones: toddler view

{2}'s screen opens in toddler view: a big monster behind a bush, giant picture buttons for his two behaviours, and a star for every Dojo today.

- Toddlers can't link a reward to something from an hour ago. Give the Dojo within seconds of the gentle touch or tidy-up.
- Let him press the giant button himself while you say the words. Pressing it and seeing the stars is the reward.
- Tap his monster for peekaboo. It's a great way to end a moment happily.
- His Dojo $ are mostly spent by grown-ups on tiny treats like a sticker or a song to dance to. Keep these quick and cheap.
- **Grown-up view** (top right) shows the full app for him if you need the shop or history.

## Fun extras to point out

| Feature | Where | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mystery eggs | The garden on the home screen | Every 10 Dojos an egg appears. Tap it three times to hatch a sticker or, sometimes, a rare monster colour. Each egg has the child's name on it, and the prize is theirs. |
| Streak fire | Next to the child's name | The flame grows for each day in a row with a Dojo. Missing a day or two only pauses it. |
| Talking monster | The big monster on the child's screen | It says hello and tells them how they're doing. Tap it to hear more. |
| Sticker book | Trophies tab | Shows every sticker found and the empty spaces still to find. |
| Garden play | Home screen | Tap a monster for a trick, tap the grass to send it somewhere, or tap two monsters for a high five or hug. Tap the sun or a cloud for a surprise. |
| Team jar | Under the garden | Everyone's Dojos fill one jar for a family treat. |
| Bedtime | The moon button in the garden | A calm, dark screen with sleepy monsters, a breathing guide and a soft lullaby. Good for winding down before bed. |
| Games | The 🎮 Play tab | Seven little games unlock as the monster levels up, including a Monster puzzle. Each one rewards speed and care, and the child chases their own high score. They never give Dojos, and a parent sets how many can be played each day. |

The garden changes with the Australian seasons and dresses up by itself for birthdays and holidays: Christmas, New Year, Lunar New Year, Valentine's Day, Hari Raya, Easter, Mother's Day, Father's Day, Mid-Autumn Festival, Deepavali and Halloween. Kites come out in the school holidays.

## What carers can do

| Carers can | Only parents can |
| --- | --- |
| Give Dojos | Spend Dojo $ on real-world treats |
| Undo their own Dojo within 15 minutes | Mark big rewards as given |
| Buy shop items with the child's Dojo $ | Change behaviours, treats, goals and settings |
| Change the monster's look, outfit and name | Invite or remove people, and back up |

If a child asks for a real-world treat or reaches a big reward while you're with them, tell them a parent will sort it out, and let a parent know.
